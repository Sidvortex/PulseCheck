🫀 PulseCheck: Lightweight AI for Early Cardiovascular Screening

Machine Learning for Low-Resource Healthcare Settings

A Cost-Effective, Accessible Approach to CVD Risk Assessment

Technical Report | December 2024 | Created by Yuan Ching

1. Executive Summary

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) remains the leading cause of global mortality, accounting for over 17.9 million deaths annually. While advanced diagnostic tools exist, they are largely inaccessible in low-resource settings where the burden of disease is highest. This project demonstrates how machine learning can enable early CVD screening using only basic clinical measurements, making preventive care accessible to underserved populations.

Key Achievement: Developed a screening model achieving 86.9% ROC-AUC using only 6 easily obtainable features—no laboratory tests, imaging, or specialized equipment required. Estimated cost per screening: $2-5 USD.

2. Problem Statement & Healthcare Context

2.1 The Global CVD Challenge

Four out of five CVD deaths occur in low- and middle-income countries where access to:

  • Laboratory testing (cholesterol panels, biomarkers) is limited or expensive
  • Specialized equipment (ECG, echocardiography) is unavailable in rural clinics
  • Trained cardiologists are concentrated in urban centers

2.2 Our Solution: Minimal-Feature Screening

This project develops a machine learning model that predicts CVD risk using only measurements obtainable by community health workers with basic equipment:

✓ Age (demographic data)
✓ Sex (demographic data)
✓ Blood Pressure (digital monitor ~$30)
✓ Heart Rate (included with BP monitor)
✓ Exercise Status (simple questionnaire)
✓ Fitness Score (derived metric)
Deliberately Excluded: Cholesterol levels, ECG, imaging, genetic testing, advanced biomarkers—all requiring laboratory infrastructure or specialized equipment.

3. Methodology

3.1 Dataset

Source: Cleveland Heart Disease Dataset from UCI Machine Learning Repository
Samples: 303 patients with 14 original features
Target: Binary classification (CVD present/absent)
Class Distribution: 46% no disease, 54% disease present

3.2 Feature Selection Rationale

Our feature selection prioritized accessibility over predictive power:

Feature Measurement Method Cost Clinical Rationale
Age Patient report $0 Primary CVD risk factor; risk increases with age
Sex Patient report $0 Males have earlier CVD onset; sex-specific risk profiles
Systolic BP Digital BP monitor $20-50 Hypertension is a major modifiable risk factor
Max Heart Rate Included with BP measurement $0 Proxy for cardiovascular fitness and stress response
Exercise Angina Patient questionnaire $0 Chest pain with exertion indicates ischemia
Fitness Score Calculated (HR/age) $0 Age-adjusted cardiovascular capacity

3.3 Preprocessing Pipeline

  • Missing value imputation using median strategy
  • Stratified train-test split (75/25) to preserve class balance
  • StandardScaler normalization for feature scaling
  • 5-fold stratified cross-validation for robust evaluation

5.2 Risk Stratification

The model categorizes patients into three risk groups:

  • High Risk (≥70% probability): 23% of patients → Immediate medical referral
  • Moderate Risk (30-70%): 44% of patients → Enhanced monitoring, lifestyle counseling
  • Low Risk (≤30%): 33% of patients → Standard preventive care
Clinical Decision Support: High-risk patients are flagged for referral to equipped healthcare facilities for comprehensive evaluation (ECG, lab tests, specialist consultation).

7. Limitations & Ethical Considerations

⚠️ Critical Limitations
  • Screening, Not Diagnosis: This tool identifies potential CVD risk but cannot diagnose cardiovascular disease. All high-risk patients require professional medical evaluation.
  • Population Specificity: Model trained on Cleveland Heart Disease dataset (predominantly Western population). Performance may vary in other demographic groups—requires local validation.
  • Temporal Validity: Patient status can change rapidly. Screening represents a snapshot, not continuous monitoring.
  • Missing Risk Factors: Important factors like cholesterol, family history, diabetes status, and lifestyle factors beyond exercise are not included.

7.1 Bias Considerations

  • Sex Bias: Dataset may underrepresent CVD in women (historically underdiagnosed)
  • Age Bias: Limited data on younger patients; model may be less accurate for age <40
  • Geographic Bias: Trained on US hospital data; may not generalize to other regions

7.2 Ethical Guidelines for Deployment

  1. Informed Consent: Patients must understand this is screening, not diagnosis
  2. Referral Pathways: Establish clear protocols for high-risk patient management
  3. Quality Assurance: Regular calibration of equipment and retraining of staff
  4. Data Privacy: Secure storage and handling of patient health information
  5. Continuous Validation: Monitor performance with local outcome data

8. Conclusion & Impact Potential

This project demonstrates that machine learning can enable accessible, cost-effective cardiovascular screening in settings where traditional diagnostic approaches are impractical. By using only six easily obtainable features, we achieve 86.9% ROC-AUC—competitive performance without laboratory infrastructure.
